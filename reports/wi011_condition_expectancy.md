## Limits (read with the tables)

- These are descriptive conditional distributions. No model is fitted, no threshold is tuned on the evaluated returns, and no multiplicity correction is applied across the 304 condition × horizon cells — the stable-sign clause is a *temporal* robustness check, not a family-wise significance test.
- Conditional means are not tradeable expectancy: they say nothing about costs, position sizing, frequency or concurrency. Step 6 applies the cost/frequency/concurrency filter; Step 5 runs the per-symbol TS-momentum probe.
- Rows where a condition is undefined (warm-up) are excluded per condition; the `n`/`dates` columns show exactly how much history each cell actually rests on.
- The purged-fold column covers only dates after the initial training block (~60% of the timeline) and is smaller by construction (embargo plus the label-end rule), so its level is not directly comparable to the full-sample mean — read it as 'does the effect still show up in the later, out-of-block sample', not as the same rows re-measured.
- **Market-beta caveat (read the `excess vs all-rows` column first).** A condition that fires disproportionately on strong market days inherits the market's drift: its raw mean bps is not a reason to trade it. The excess column subtracts the all-rows mean for the same horizon, and Step 5 decomposes each condition's forward return into a same-day market component and a within-date cross-sectional deviation, which is the cleaner 'which name' question.
